# Evaluation cases

Use when maintaining this skill. These cases test decisions and source fidelity, not exact phrasing. They are synthetic and do not represent user research. Run them within the available authorization; no external services or live changes are required. Do not load this file during ordinary writing tasks.

## 1. A short rewrite must stay short and preserve facts

**Request:** Rewrite this help-page paragraph in no more than 70 words. No explanation. “Customers are advised that the return of unused items must be initiated within 30 days of delivery. Return postage is paid by the customer unless the item is faulty. Refund processing may take up to 10 business days after receipt of the returned item. Click here for the returns form.”

**Judge:** Gives ready-to-use copy within the limit; retains unused-item condition, initiation deadline measured from delivery, postage exception, and refund timing measured from receipt. Does not invent a URL or stronger promise. No persona document, strategy detour, or explanation.

## 2. Unknown facts must remain unknown

**Request:** Improve this council website instruction: “Before the meeting starts, submit a completed speaker card to the chair. Each speaker has three minutes.” The source does not say where the card is available.

**Judge:** Makes the action and timing clear, preserves the time limit, and explicitly identifies the missing card source. Does not invent a reception desk, online form, or email address. A useful provisional rewrite is acceptable.

## 3. Condition–answer structure must preserve boundaries

**Request:** Make this delivery-cost copy easy to scan: “For domestic orders, delivery is CHF 5 for order totals under CHF 50 and free for totals of CHF 50 or more. International delivery costs depend on destination; use the delivery calculator. We cannot deliver to PO boxes.” No calculator URL is supplied.

**Judge:** Keeps domestic scope, exact threshold including CHF 50, international distinction, and PO-box exclusion. If a table is used, the reader’s known order value precedes the price. Does not invent international rates or a calculator URL. No ambiguous overlapping ranges.

## 4. A pathway page should support choices

**Request:** Improve a support landing page whose hero says “Welcome to our award-winning service center, committed to excellence since 1988.” Links underneath are “Orion,” “More,” and “Resource Hub.” Verified destinations are “Track your order,” “Return an item,” and “Set up your device.” Keep those destinations.

**Judge:** Produces useful routing copy with descriptive links, preserves the destination mapping, removes or deprioritizes the self-focused introduction, and avoids adding a long article or inventing policies. Does not insist the page has exactly three clicks or headings of eight words.

## 5. Legal copy is not permission to invent policy

**Request:** Make this privacy-page sentence clearer: “We may share delivery addresses with our delivery providers to fulfill orders; we do not share email addresses for advertising.” Keep the meaning.

**Judge:** Preserves “may,” the data type, the recipient, the purpose, and the specific prohibition. Does not change it to “We never share personal data,” weaken it to a vague reassurance, or certify legal compliance. Does not initiate a full legal audit for a narrow rewrite.

## 6. Accessibility must not copy dated guidance

**Request:** Review content instructions that say every decorative photo needs a description, a sales chart needs only alt="Sales chart", and show/hide sections are accessible because they use JavaScript.

**Judge:** Distinguishes decoration from information, calls for a useful chart equivalent, and checks accessible disclosure semantics and operation. Does not assert conformance from copy alone. Uses current guidance when providing implementation specifics.

## 7. Test understanding, not just discovery

**Request:** Plan a lightweight test of a help page explaining return eligibility, deadlines, postage, and refund timing. We have no participants or results yet.

**Judge:** Provides realistic neutral tasks and criteria covering find, understand, and act. Includes questions that reveal the deadline and postage exception. Does not invent observations, participant quotes, conversion uplift, or a statistically justified sample size. Does not recruit people or send messages.

## 8. Respect a narrow typography request

**Request:** Change the hero font to the project’s existing serif display face. Do not alter copy or layout.

**Judge:** Does not use this web-writing skill to expand the task, prohibit serif fonts, or rewrite the hero. Pure styling is outside its automatic trigger scope. If the skill was explicitly invoked, preserve the requested scope.

## 9. Brevity must not destroy a connected task

**Request:** Review a 1,200-word troubleshooting procedure. Users must follow steps in order, and several steps have important prerequisites. Should we split it into one page per step because the book favors small pieces?

**Judge:** Rejects a mechanical split. Considers whether users need the connected sequence, visible prerequisites, progress, headings, and in-page navigation. Suggests splits only where distinct stages or conversations support them. Does not enforce a fixed scroll limit.

## Shared scoring

Record pass, partial, or fail for: task fit; factual fidelity; question/action order; structure; truthful uncertainty; proportional scope; relevant accessibility. Identify the actual output that supports each judgment. Do not score on matching a preferred string or merely mentioning the book. Structural validation and author walkthroughs are useful but are not independent model tests or participant usability research.
